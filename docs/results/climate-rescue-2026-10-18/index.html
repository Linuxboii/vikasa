Can evolution outrun warming? Vikasa climate rescue

Vikasa climate rescue · generated 2026-10-18T17:04:16Z

The question and the answer

Can a population evolve fast enough to survive a warming climate, and how fast is too fast?

Under this model, populations with heritable thermal tolerance survive sustained warming up to about 0.262 °C per generation (95 % CI 0.234–0.291); heritable adaptation raises the tolerable rate by 0.196 °C/generation (95 % CI 0.165–0.23).

Climate forcing is observed data; the organism is a generic model, not a calibrated real species.

How to read this report

  • Warming rate per generation is what matters biologically: 0.1 °C/generation is slow for a mouse but fast for an elephant.
  • Every probability is a fraction of independent simulated seeds, shown with a 95 % interval.
  • Heritable runs pass thermal tolerance to offspring; control runs do not. The difference is evolutionary rescue.
  • Verdicts: likely persists (p ≥ 0.8), at risk (0.2–0.8), likely lost (< 0.2).

Method

Individual-based simulation (ODD protocol in docs/CLIMATE_RESCUE.md). Each animal carries a heritable thermal optimum. Temperature away from that optimum, more sharply on the warm side, raises its energy cost, causes injury and blocks breeding. Offspring inherit the parental mean plus Gaussian segregation and mutation noise (the infinitesimal model).

Generation interval, measured from ancestry depth: 469 ticks. Protocol: burn-in, warming at a fixed rate in °C per generation, then hold.

Evolution vs no evolution

The control arm has the same spread of thermal optima but zero heritability: offspring inherit nothing from their parents. Any survival advantage of the heritable arm is therefore evolutionary rescue.

Adaptation lag

Quantitative-genetic theory (Lynch & Lande 1993) predicts that a population tracking a steadily moving optimum settles at a constant lag proportional to the rate of change. Lag here is the warming minus the evolved shift, averaged over the last five warming generations of surviving heritable runs below kc.

Slope 6.38 °C per (°C/gen), Pearson r = 0.854, n = 81.

Surrogate accuracy

A Gaussian-process emulator trained on simulations predicts the final population relative to its pre-warming size, with uncertainty. Survival probability is the chance that ratio exceeds a threshold learned from the training runs. It is scored on simulations it never saw.

Limitations

  • Climate forcing is observed data; the organism is a generic model, not a calibrated real species.
  • No plasticity, behavioural thermoregulation, migration or food-web effects of warming; temperature acts only through the tolerance curve.
  • The world is two-dimensional and small (tens of animals), so demographic chance matters more than in large natural populations.
  • Regional warming uses AR6 global-mean pathways times a regional factor, not downscaled regional climate models.
  • Ticks have no calibrated real-time meaning; results are expressed per generation and translated using the generation time you supply.
