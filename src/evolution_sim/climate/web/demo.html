Chapter 1 · The question

Which species will climate change wipe out, and which can evolve fast enough to survive?

Vikasa is an individual-based evolution simulator. Each animal inherits genes, and those genes decide how it lives. We pair it with real NASA temperature records for Indian places and the IPCC's warming scenarios.

Chapter 2 · Watch evolution happen

A world you can watch like a nature film

Autopilot mode flies the camera, finds fights, births and families, captions them, and brings in night and a storm. Press any key in the 3D window to take control.

Point out

  • Every animal decides for itself. Click one to see its action and the reason behind it: hunger, danger, a mate, its young.
  • Genes are visible. Size, ears, ridges and tail come from inherited genes. Press V to recolour by dynasty or generation.
  • Day and night, weather, sound. All procedural: no audio files, a 26,000-blade grass field, the whole population drawn in two GPU draw calls.
  • Stress it. Press T, apply a storm, and watch vulnerable animals die while survivors carry on.
Chapter 3 · Turn up the heat

Real seasons. Rising temperature. Natural selection live.

The Western Ghats' monthly temperatures from NASA, with steady warming added. Each animal carries a heat-tolerance gene, and you can watch the population chase a moving target.

Point out

  • The live °C in the top bar. Hover it to see the adaptation lag: how far the population's average comfort temperature trails the climate.
  • "Below a critical speed, evolution keeps pace with a constant lag. Above it, the lag grows until the population dies out. That's textbook theory from 1993, and our simulation reproduces it."

Cheat sheet

Likely questions

Is this a real species?

No. The climate is real data; the organism is a generic model. The Risk Checker lets you enter a real species' generation time and heat tolerance, and calibrating to Western Ghats frogs and lizards is the next step.

How do you know the simulation is right?

It reproduces established theory (Lynch & Lande 1993): the adaptation lag grows linearly with warming rate (r = 0.85), and survival drops sharply at a critical rate. Success criteria were fixed before running, and the failures are published.

What's actually new here?

Not the theory. The novelty is combining a watchable, explainable individual-based simulation with real climate records, IPCC scenarios, sensitivity analysis and a decision tool in one reproducible package.

Why does the instant estimate differ from the live check?

The estimate uses the survival curve measured for one reference organism. The live check simulates your exact place and tolerance directly. When they differ, the live check is the answer, and the app labels which is which.

Who would use this?

Biology teachers showing evolution in action; students exploring climate risk for their own city; and, once calibrated to real species, conservation planners screening which local species need attention first.

What would you build next?

Real species data (GlobTherm heat tolerances, GBIF ranges), downscaled CMIP6 projections, and shade-seeking and migration behaviour. Those three are the model's main stated limitations.
